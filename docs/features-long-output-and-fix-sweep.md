# Long output and the fix sweep: the asleep pane, the jump button, the Wake box, the visual fixes, the thread's own folder, the project icon

The full record of Phase 9 and the follow-ups from Aryan's first days on the build (2026-09-19 and 2026-09-20), moved here from CLAUDE.md on 2026-09-20. CLAUDE.md's "How the app works today" section is the short version.

### Long output, the fix sweep, the thread's own folder in Explorer, the project icon everywhere (Phase 9)

Phase 9 is Clusters 2 and 3 of `docs/plan-01-sidebar-attention-and-manual-testing-fixes.md`, the last phase before 0.9.0: every entry in `docs/bugs.md` from the manual-testing round closed in one pass, plus two additions Aryan asked for mid-phase. Five decisions were his, taken on 2026-09-19 before any code: the dimmed snapshot is dropped on wake; one arrow button that flips with the scroll direction; the header line gets brighter text at the same size (chosen from `docs/mockups/header-meta-variants.html`, the current line and the three treatments side by side); the notifier bug is fixed now; the "Last here" line stays open, he decides later, so it is left exactly as built and not asked about again.

**The asleep pane opens at its newest lines.** `AsleepPane/index.tsx` scrolls its scroller to the end whenever the `tail` prop lands or the tab changes. The scroller is an inner `.asleep-scroll` now (the outer `.asleep-pane` only sizes and clips the card, exactly the relative-host/absolute-child shape `.terminal-instances` uses for its find bar), which is what lets the jump button sit as a sibling and stay put. The scroll-to-end has a second trigger, a `ResizeObserver` on the scroller: the pane can mount while the workspace is hidden (Home or a project page showing over it, the workspace kept mounted with `display: none`), and a hidden scroller has no `scrollHeight` to scroll to, so the effect alone landed at 0 (found in the harness). A `needsEndRef` flag keeps the intent and the observer finishes the job the moment the pane gets a size. Since the sticky Wake box now sits over dimmed lines rather than above them, Aryan's rule (2026-09-20, after a radial "clearing" and then a frosted card were tried and rejected): the white Wake button carries a thin border in the pane's colour (`--pane`, new in theme.css, the terminal card and pane surface) and the "asleep since" line sits in a solid pill of that colour, on the palette tokens like everything else in the app; the jump button's disc and arrow are `--hover2` and `--text2` the same way. The pane also keeps following its end through the sidebar's slide-in (`followEndRef`, cleared by a user scroll that leaves the end), since the reflow after the scroll-to-end left it short.

**The wake replay is gone.** `createTerminal` in `Terminal/index.tsx` no longer reads the tail file or writes it into the fresh xterm, and the scroll-back block that followed ConPTY's first paint went with it: a woken thread opens clean at its prompt, with nothing dimmed in the scrollback, because the asleep pane has already shown the same tail and the replay used to stay on screen after the terminal came back (Aryan's decision, plan-01 question 11). `renderTailForTerminal` and its tests were removed from `src/thread-tail.ts`; `Tab.wokeAt` stays as the transient "woken this launch" stamp, since it is woven through `sleepWake.ts`, `history.ts` and the migration and costs nothing, but nothing in the terminal reads it any more.

**The jump button.** One small round chevron per scroller (`components/JumpButton`), the live terminal and the asleep pane, floating at the scroller's exact centre, horizontally and vertically (Aryan, 2026-09-19: the wheel is used in the middle of the pane, so the button belongs where the pointer already is, not at an edge), that appears while the user is scrolled away from the end they are heading toward and points that way: scrolling up offers "Go to top", scrolling down offers "Go to bottom", the wording Aryan used in the bug entry. The rule is pure (`src/renderer/jumpScroll.ts`, `onScrollSample(state, position, max, threshold)`, 32 checks): scrolled up shows `'top'` when `position > threshold`, scrolled down shows `'bottom'` when `max - position > threshold`, a resample with no movement keeps the current target but drops it once its own end is within the threshold, and `max <= 0` is always hidden. That last two cover Aryan's edge cases: already at the bottom and scrolling further shows nothing; a little way from the bottom and scrolling down onto it shows nothing; new output arriving while following the bottom (position and max grow together, remaining 0) shows nothing; a click on the button lands at an end and hides it. The terminal samples xterm's `buffer.active.viewportY` (position) and `baseY` (max) on `term.onScroll` with `JUMP_THRESHOLD_LINES` (3) and keeps one `JumpState` per tab in `jumpRef`, re-based on every thread switch so the next scroll's direction is read against where that thread's viewport is now; the pane samples its div's `scrollTop` and `scrollHeight - clientHeight` with `JUMP_THRESHOLD_PX` (48). The button prevents mousedown's default so it never takes focus from the terminal, carries no tooltip (Aryan: no hover text over the spot the wheel is used), and comes and goes by scaling only, no fade (`data-shown` drives a `scale(0)` to `scale(1)` transition on `--med`, with `visibility` flipping after the exit so a hidden button is never hoverable; it stays mounted while it shrinks away, keeping the last target for its icon). Because it sits under the pointer mid-scroll, a wheel event over it is handed back to the host through `onWheel` and applied to the scroller: the pane calls `scrollBy`, the terminal calls `term.scrollLines` with lines computed by `wheelToLines` in `jumpScroll.ts` (pixels over the cell height, deltas under 50px damped to 30%, the fractional remainder carried over, exactly xterm's own `consumeWheelEvent` maths; replaying a constructed `WheelEvent` onto xterm does nothing, since xterm 6 reads the legacy `wheelDeltaY` field, which a constructed event leaves at 0). The wheel scroll itself is untouched, only the click animates. A jump scrolls, it does not teleport (Aryan, 2026-09-19): the pane uses the browser's own `scrollTo({ behavior: 'smooth' })`; xterm has no smooth scrolling, so the terminal moves its viewport line by line on animation frames along an ease-out cubic (`jumpDurationMs`, 180ms plus 2ms per line capped at 450ms, and `jumpLineAt` in `jumpScroll.ts`, both pure and tested), re-reading the bottom on every frame so output arriving mid-jump is still reached, cancelling a jump in flight on a second click, and stopping if the terminal is torn down mid-jump. Both are instant under reduced motion (`prefersReducedMotion()` in `jumpScroll.ts`, the one place both hosts ask), and the button's fade is off through theme.css's global rule. The harness reads the viewport through `window.__afterterm.viewport(tabId?)` and `scripts/agent-harness/watch-jump.mjs` samples it through a jump.

**The fix sweep.** `.header-meta` values move from `--text3` to `--text2` at the same 12.5px, with the item's own svg kept dim so the words lead (the rule targets direct svg children only, so the project icon inside `.fo` keeps the colour FolderIcon sets inline; the first cut targeted every svg and overrode it). `.notif-card`'s shadow is `0 2px 6px` plus `0 4px 7px`, under the overlay's 12px padding on every side, so nothing is clipped at the window edge (verified with a whole-display capture, since CDP cannot see the overlay window). `.hover-card .hn` clamps to two lines with an ellipsis and breaks anywhere. The notifier overlay follows the main window: `notifierDisplay()` in `main.ts` returns `screen.getDisplayMatching(mainWindow.getBounds())`, read fresh on every placement (a push re-places before `showInactive`, the renderer's resize report re-places, and `display-added`, `display-removed` and `display-metrics-changed` re-place at once), and `AFTERTERM_DISPLAY` only applies before the main window exists, so the harness, whose main window sits on the override's display, exercises this exact path. Verified on the secondary display only: the primary case would put a dev build on the monitor Aryan is using, so it is the same call with a different window position, read from the code, not run.

**Open in File Explorer for a thread's own folder.** `threadFolder(tab)` in `threadView.ts` is the thread's working folder: the hook-reported `claudeCwd` for a chat (often a worktree), the shell's `cwd` otherwise, undefined when neither was captured; `threadGitCwd` in `useTabState.ts` is now an alias of it, so the branch, the worktree and Explorer all read the same folder. The one thread menu (`threadMenu.tsx`) takes `openInExplorer?: { missing, open }`: absent when the thread has no folder (the item is left out), disabled with the project menu's own "Folder not found" tip when the folder is missing. `app.tsx`'s `threadExplorer(tab)` builds it for the sidebar right-click (through a `threadExplorer` prop on `SidePanel`), the header dots menu and the project page rows, and `openFolderInExplorer` is the one launch the project menu and the thread menu share (the same `projects:openInExplorer` IPC, the same toast on failure). The header's worktree item is a button (`.header-meta-link`) that opens the same folder, the entry's first ask, and the project item next to it opens the project's folder (`projectExplorer` prop on `Header`, built in `app.tsx` from the active group's `cwd` and `folderMissing`; Aryan, 2026-09-19). Under the harness both only log unless the dev build was launched with `launch.mjs --open-external` (`AFTERTERM_OPEN_EXTERNAL=1`, `harnessOnlyLogsExternal()` in `main.ts`), which is how the replica left for Aryan gets real Explorer and browser launches; his first replica had none and the entries looked broken. Folder checks (`projects:checkFolders`) now cover thread folders too and re-run whenever the set of folders changes (a thread cd-ing somewhere new), which keeps the workspace's thread menus honest without polling. Under the harness main logs `[harness] projects:openInExplorer <folder>` instead of launching Explorer, and the folder is recorded on `window.__afterterm.lastOpenFolder`, which `drive opened` prints.

**The project icon everywhere.** Phase 8 showed a project's chosen icon on the rail tile and the sidebar row; Aryan asked on 2026-09-19 for it to replace the folder glyph everywhere a project is drawn, with the folder as the default when none is chosen. Every `FolderIcon` that draws a project now passes `icon={group.icon}`: Home cards and rows, the header, the hover card, the chooser, the palette, the project page, the Move to project submenu; the overlay toast carries `projectIcon` in its payload and draws `ProjectIcon` in the project's colour when the id is valid.

**A pinned pin answers the pointer.** On a Home card the pin button of a pinned project is already at full colour and the hovered card is already `--hover2`, so the shared `.ib:hover` changed nothing visible (Aryan, 2026-09-19: it should react like the unpinned one does). `.ib.on:hover` in theme.css gets a lighter fill and a small lift, the lift off under reduced motion.

**Only the user's own scrolling shows the button** (found by Aryan on 2026-09-19 in his first days on the build: the button was there the moment an asleep thread opened). A scroller also moves on its own: opening a thread slides the sidebar in, the pane narrows, long tail lines re-wrap and the browser's scroll anchoring shifts `scrollTop`; the pane's scroll-to-end, the animated jump and xterm's scroll-to-bottom on input move it too, and every one of those fires scroll events. So each host stamps the time of the last user input that scrolls (a wheel or a key on the scroller, the wheel handed on from the button) and holds a flag while a scrollbar drag is in progress (a pointer down on the pane's scroller itself rather than its content, or outside `.xterm-screen` in the terminal, released on `pointerup` anywhere), and `isUserScroll` in `jumpScroll.ts` (a 250ms window, or the drag flag) decides whether a scroll event is sampled through `onScrollSample` or only updates the sampled position. A click on the button hides it at once (`initialJumpState` at the destination) so the jump's own scroll events never re-show it. Verified on a real 200-line tail: toggling the sidebar re-wrapped the tail (max 4343 to 4156 and back) with the button staying hidden.

**The scrollbar corner.** theme.css paints `::-webkit-scrollbar-corner` transparent (Chromium's default is white, seen as a white square where two bars met on the asleep pane on 2026-09-19), and `.asleep-scroll` clips horizontal overflow, since the Wake box backdrop reaches past the content width on purpose and was producing a horizontal bar. Both scrollbars also stop short of the card's rounded corners (2026-09-20): the pane's native track gets a `margin` of the surface radius, and xterm 6's own scrollbar (`.xterm-scrollable-element > .scrollbar.vertical`, positioned from JS, so it is clipped rather than resized) gets a rounded `clip-path` inset that starts and ends where the card's straight edge does; the 8px top and 16px bottom insets follow from the container's 10px padding, the card's 2px inset and the bar's element overhanging the `.xterm` box to the card's bottom edge.

**Harness.** `drive scroll <selector> <deltaY>` sends wheel events over an element in 100px steps (xterm turns each into a line count, so one huge event would jump further than a wheel can); `drive jump` reads the button's state and target; `drive pane-scroll` reads the pane's `scrollTop`, max and `atEnd`, and `drive pane` prints the same line. One test mistake worth knowing: Git's coreutils `timeout` shadows cmd's in the harness shells, so `timeout /t N` fails at once and a scheduled title lands while the thread is still being viewed; `ping -n N 127.0.0.1 >nul` is the delay to use.
